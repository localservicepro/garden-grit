Tree pruning Adelaide homeowners can trust: pruning, not lopping

Lopping is cutting a tree back to stubs to make it smaller. It looks tidy for a month and then produces a thicket of weak, fast regrowth that is worse than what you started with. Proper tree pruning removes the right branches, at the right point, at the right time of year, so the tree stays strong, lets light into the garden and keeps a natural shape. That is the difference a qualified horticulturist makes.

What I prune

  • Fruit trees: apples, pears, plums, apricots, citrus, figs and olives, pruned for fruit and for picking height
  • Ornamental trees: Crepe Myrtles, Japanese Maples, Magnolias, Robinias, Prunus and flowering fruit trees
  • Formative pruning of young trees so they develop a sound structure
  • Crown lifting and thinning to let light into lawns and beds underneath
  • Deadwood removal, crossing branches and clearance from gutters, paths and fences
  • Small tree and shrub removal, and stump removal where access allows

Work is limited to small and medium trees that can be pruned safely from the ground or a ladder. Large trees, trees near power lines or anything needing a climber or a chipper are referred to a qualified arborist, and I will tell you up front if that is what your tree needs.

Tree trimming Adelaide: timing matters

Deciduous fruit trees are pruned in winter when they are dormant; stone fruit are better done in late summer after harvest to avoid disease. Crepe Myrtles are cut in late winter, Magnolias straight after flowering, and citrus only lightly, after the frost risk has passed. If you are not sure when your tree should be cut, ask. I have written a month-by-month guide to pruning fruit trees in South Australia.

What to expect on the day

I start by walking around the tree and explaining which cuts I plan to make and why: a crossing branch here, a water shoot there, lifting the lower limbs so you can mow underneath. Cuts are made just outside the branch collar so the tree can seal them, never flush and never left as stubs. Large reductions are spread over more than one season so the tree is not shocked into a flush of weak regrowth. Everything is raked up and taken away, and if the tree had disease, the tools are sterilised between trees so it does not travel through your garden. For fruit trees you also get a quick word on feeding and watering so the pruning actually turns into fruit.

Tree Pruning: common questions

How much does tree pruning cost in Adelaide?

Small ornamental and fruit tree pruning is quoted per tree or per visit after I have seen the tree. Size, access, how long since it was last pruned and how much green waste there is to remove are what move the price. Every job is a fixed quote, not an hourly guess.

When is the best time to prune fruit trees in Adelaide?

Pome fruit (apples, pears) in winter while dormant; stone fruit (apricots, plums, peaches) in late summer after fruiting, to reduce disease; citrus lightly in spring after the last frost. Evergreen ornamentals can usually be pruned in late winter or after flowering.

Do you remove trees?

Small trees and shrubs, yes, including stump removal where access allows. Large tree removal is arborist work, and some trees in South Australia are protected under the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act, so always check before removing a significant tree.

Will pruning make my tree smaller?

Pruning can reduce size gradually and keep a tree in proportion, but a tree cannot be made permanently small by cutting it back hard. If a tree is simply too big for its spot, I will say so and talk through options.

Do you work in Magill, Campbelltown and Athelstone?

Yes. Those north-eastern suburbs are a regular run from Norwood, along with Paradise, Dernancourt, Modbury and Tea Tree Gully.
