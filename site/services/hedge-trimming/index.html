Hedge trimming Adelaide gardens actually look better for

A hedge cut square and level is a simple thing that is surprisingly rare. It takes a string line, a steady hand and some knowledge of how each species regrows. A Lilly Pilly or Viburnum will take a hard cut and bounce back; a Conifer or a Lavender hedge cut into old wood will not recover. As a qualified horticulturist, I trim with the plant's growth habit and flowering time in mind, so the hedge gets tighter and healthier each season rather than thinner.

What is included in a hedge trim

  • Formal hedges trimmed level and plumb with a string line, faces tapered so light reaches the base
  • Informal and screening hedges shaped to keep them dense and within bounds
  • Rejuvenation pruning for overgrown or leggy hedges, staged over a season or two where needed
  • Topiary balls, cloud pruning and feature plants
  • All clippings raked, blown and removed

Common hedges in the eastern suburbs include Lilly Pilly (Syzygium), Photinia, Viburnum, Murraya, Buxus, Pittosporum and Westringia. Each has a best time to cut and a limit to how hard it can be cut, and I will tell you which applies to yours.

Hedge pruning for homes, strata and body corporates

The long boundary hedges of Walkerville, Prospect, Medindie and Toorak Gardens are a big part of those streets' character. I handle single-home hedges and the shared hedges, screens and street frontages that strata groups and body corporates in Norwood, Kent Town and St Peters need kept presentable, on a regular schedule if you prefer.

Why the first cut after a long gap is different

If a hedge has not been trimmed for a year or more, the first visit is about restoring the shape rather than just removing the fluff. That means finding the original line, cutting back to it in stages so the hedge is never stripped bare, and feeding and watering afterwards so it refills quickly. The second and third trims that year are quick, and by the following season the hedge is back on a normal schedule. I will tell you at the quote which category your hedge is in.

Hedges along driveways and footpaths in Norwood, Walkerville and Prospect also have a sight-line and council-clearance side to them. Keeping the hedge back from the footpath and below the height that blocks a driver's view is part of the trim, not an extra.

How often should a hedge be trimmed?

In Adelaide's climate, most formal hedges want two or three trims a year: one in late winter before the spring flush, one in early summer, and often a tidy-up in autumn. Fast growers like Photinia and Lilly Pilly can take a fourth. I cover the reasoning in how often to trim a hedge in Adelaide's climate.

Hedge Pruning & Trimming: common questions

How often should hedges be trimmed in Adelaide's climate?

Two to three times a year for most formal hedges: late winter, early summer and an autumn tidy. Fast-growing hedges such as Photinia or Lilly Pilly may need four. Flowering hedges are trimmed straight after flowering so you do not lose next year's display.

Can you bring an overgrown hedge back under control?

Usually, yes. Most broadleaf hedges can be cut back hard and will regrow, though it is often staged over two seasons so the hedge never looks bare. Conifers and some others cannot be cut into old wood, so I will tell you honestly what is possible.

Do you trim tall hedges?

Yes, hedges up to a safe working height from a platform or ladder. Very tall screening trees are treated as tree pruning rather than hedging.

Do you take the clippings away?

Yes. Every hedge trim includes clean up and removal of the clippings.

Which suburbs do you cover for hedge trimming?

Norwood and the eastern suburbs, including Walkerville, Prospect, Toorak Gardens, Burnside, Magill, St Peters and north to Tea Tree Gully.
